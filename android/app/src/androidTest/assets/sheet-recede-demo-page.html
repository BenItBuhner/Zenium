Sheets over a page: the recede

A demo page served from inside the browser's own process. Nothing here is fetched from the network.

When a sheet comes up over this page the page moves back a little: it scales to 97 percent about its centre and its corners round off by six pixels more, while a scrim darkens it and the bottom bar fades. All of that runs on one value, the sheet's own progress, so a sheet dragged halfway has the page halfway back, and letting go settles both on the same spring.

The live page and its picture must never be told apart. Before the sheet rises the page is photographed and the picture takes its place at the same size and position; only then does anything move. On the way out the picture stays until the page has been drawn again where the picture was, and only then does it go. A frame that shows the window behind the page, or a frame in which the page jumps in brightness, is a failure of that promise.

What the camera measures

The upper part of the page never has a sheet over it. Its mean brightness should fall as the scrim fades in and rise as the scrim fades out, smoothly, and it should look like a page in every frame: grey and white pixels, with the edges of letters, never the smooth tinted gradient of the window.

Second sheetStackedSystem back, twice

Two sheets at once make a stack of depth two. The sheet on top owns the one scrim; the sheet beneath it recedes the way the page did for the first, and takes no input while it is under. The page itself does not move again for the second sheet.

Reduced motion

With reduced motion on, the page does not recede and the bar does not fade; a sheet arrives in place and its scrim fades in over a short time. The same values are still written each frame; their gain is simply zero.
